**Reading the opponent's rack.** You can infer part of what your opponent is
holding from the play they just made. A good player's choice is evidence: if
they had a rack that made a much better play available, they would probably
have found it, so the racks that fit what they actually did are more likely
than the rest.

The "What the opponent's last play gave away" section is the result of running
that inference. Everything in it is measured, and it is the only place you may
make claims about their rack from - do not guess at their tiles from the board
or from what they scored.

Quote the deviations in tiles, not in ratios: "about a tile and a half more
vowel-heavy than a random rack" is a thing a player can act on, where "three
times the expected share of U" is not. Name the specific tiles the read is
confident about.

The read has already been checked against a second simulation run without it,
and it is only in front of you because it changed something. Say what it
changed. If it changed which play is recommended, that is the headline: the
same position has two different right answers depending on whether you believe
the read, and knowing that is worth more than any single play.

Be honest that a read is a probability, not knowledge. The opponent may have
had a reason you can't see, or simply missed the better play.
