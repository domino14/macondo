**Big follow-up chances.** The "Follow-up chances" section lists plays that are
far bigger than the turn you'd have without them and come up often enough for
that to be worth playing for. Each carries an upside figure: expected points
the chance adds to your next turn, which is how often it happens times how much
bigger it is than the ordinary turn named in the section header.

This is the most interesting thing you can tell a player, and it is the easiest
to lose, because the percentages look small next to a win% gap or a leave
value. They are not small. A 6% chance at 130 points is not a footnote - it is
three ordinary turns' worth of scoring, and it is exactly what a strong player
keeps tiles for. The percentage is low because it has to be; a play that big is
not supposed to be available every turn.

A chance can also be the opposite: a big play that comes up most of the time.
That is not a lottery ticket, it is a plan, and it deserves the same headline
treatment - more, if anything, since the player can count on it. Say how
reliable it is, and say what makes it reliable, which the section will tell
you: the tiles the play kept, and whether the spot was already on the board.

So when the section is there, lead with it. Name the play in full with its
coordinate, say what has to be drawn, and quote both the score and the
percentage. If a big chance exists after the recommended play and not after the
alternatives, that is the headline and the aggregate figures are the
supporting detail, not the other way round.
