sim [args] [options] - Start or stop a Monte Carlo simulation

Example:
    sim
    sim -plies 3
    sim -plies 3 -stop 95
    sim -plies 3 -threads 3
    sim continue
    sim stop
    sim show
    sim details
    sim log
    sim trim 3
    sim -opprack AENST
    sim -collect-heatmap true
    sim heatmap "F3 BAR" 0
    sim playstats "H3 FOO"
    sim tilestats
    sim -useinferences weightedrandomracks
    sim -endgame-leaves false
    sim -stop 99 -stoprule paired
    sim -stop 99 -stoprule bai -stopcap 1000

A list of plays must have been generated or added in another way already.

Optional arguments:

    Use `stop` to stop a running simulation, `show` to show the plays ranked
    by equity so far, and `details` to see more per-ply details of each play.

    Sim `continue` will continue a previously stopped simulation from where
    it left off. You can `stop` a simulation, delete plays with the `trim`
    command, and restart the simulation this way if you wish it to be faster.

    Sim `trim` takes in a numerical argument: the number of plays to remove
    from the BOTTOM of the sorted play list. So, for example, `sim trim 5`
    will remove the bottom 5 plays. You may want to run this after simulating
    for some number of iterations.

    Before starting a simulation, you can also do `sim log` to write the log
    to a temporary file.

    Use `heatmap` as an argument to display a heatmap of the last sim for a
    particular play.
    You must have set the `-collect-heatmap` option to `true` in order to do this.
    This argument also requires two additional arguments: the play, and the
    ply index (starting at 0). For example:

    sim heatmap "H3 FOO" 0

    A ply index of 0 corresponds to your opponent's response. If a ply index is
    not provided, it defaults to 0. Ply index of 1 is your next move after
    your opponent's response, and so on.

    sim playstats "H3 FOO" shows detailed stats for a specific play. This play
    must have been simmed, and the `-collect-heatmap` option must have been set to
    `true`.

    sim playstats "H3 FOO" histogram -- this additionally shows a histogram of score
    distributions for the next two plies.

    sim tilestats shows stats about your opponent's tiles - what the percentage
    chance is of them having a certain tile. It doesn't use inference for this,
    just Monte Carlo, at this time. See `help infer` if you would like to use
    inference instead.

Options:

    -plies 3

    The above option will use 3 plies of look-ahead. By default, Macondo
    uses 2 plies.

    -threads 3

    The above option sets the number of threads that Macondo uses to sim
    your game (3 in this example). If not set, this number defaults to
    the greater or 1 or your number of CPUs minus 1.

    -stop 95

    You can use 95, 98, or 99 for this option. This makes the sim stop when it is
    X percent sure that the top move is the winning move.

    With -stop the sim uses best-arm identification (bai) unless -stoprule
    says otherwise; see -stoprule below for what that means. A sim that has
    not told the plays apart also stops when its budget of rollouts runs
    out (-stopcap), which usually means the top plays are about as good as
    each other.

    -opprack AENST

    You can specify the opponent's rack (or partial rack) if you know it, for a
    more accurate sim. Use ? for blanks.

    -useinferences weightedrandomracks

    You can use automatic inferences while simming. You must have run the
    `infer` command prior to using this. There are two options for inference
    mode:

        weightedrandomtiles:
            Inference returns a number of racks with a "weight" attached to them.
            This mode computes a tile distribution based on all of the racks
            and randomly draws from this distribution for the opponent's
            inferred rack.

        weightedrandomracks:
            This simpler mode just draws the racks themselves with the given
            weights. It does not draw tile-by-tile. This mode might perform better.

    Note: the opprack option is not compatible with this. If you use both,
    it will ignore the opprack.

    -stoprule bai

    With -stop, how the sim chooses what to roll out and when to stop: one
    of bai (the default), classic and paired.

        bai:
            Best-arm identification, the method MAGPIE uses, and the
            default. The question it asks is only "which play is best?",
            so it spends its rollouts where that is in doubt. Every play is
            first rolled out 128 times. After that the sim rolls out mostly
            the leading play and its closest challenger, and it stops when
            the leader has been told apart from every other play at the
            -stop confidence, or when the budget (-stopcap) or the time
            (-maxtime) runs out. A play's n-th rollout gets the same
            opponent rack and draws as every other play's n-th rollout, so
            plays are compared on the same luck.

            At 5 plies it picks plays as good as the classic rule's in
            about 30% less time. At 2 plies it is no faster than classic,
            and can be slower: its rollouts cost more, because the move
            generator cannot reuse its work across the plays of an
            iteration.

            Reading the results: the leaders have many rollouts and tight
            numbers; a play the sim lost interest in may have only its
            first 128, so its win % and equity are rough. That is by
            design. To get good numbers for a particular play, name it
            with -avoid-prune: when the sim stops, that play is rolled out
            until it has as many rollouts as the most rolled out play.

        classic:
            Every iteration deals the opponent a rack and rolls out every
            play still in the sim against it. A play is dropped when its
            confidence interval lies below the leader's. All surviving
            plays get the same number of rollouts.

        paired:
            The same iterations as classic, but a play is dropped by a
            test on its differences with the leader, iteration by
            iteration. The rollouts of an iteration share their luck, so a
            difference is much less noisy than either play's own results,
            and plays are told apart in fewer rollouts. Nothing is dropped
            in the first 128 iterations.

    A sim without -stop runs until you stop it, rolling every play out
    equally; -stoprule has no effect on it.

    -stopcap 1000

    With -stoprule paired or bai, the most rollouts the sim makes, as an
    average per play (so 1000 with 50 plays is 50,000 rollouts, most of
    them of the leaders). The default is 1500; past that the plays picked
    barely improve.

    -maxtime 30

    Stop the sim after this many seconds if it has not stopped by itself;
    the leader at that point is the pick. By default there is no limit.

    -bagcycle false

    With -stoprule bai, a sample of a play is by default not one rollout
    but a cycle of them: the unseen tiles are shuffled once and dealt to
    the opponent seven at a time, so every unseen tile lands on the
    opponent's rack once in the cycle. This takes the luck of the
    opponent's draw out of each sample, and the sim reaches the same picks
    in somewhat fewer rollouts. It is not used when the opponent's rack is
    known or inferred. This option turns it off.

    -endgame-leaves false

    With one or two tiles in the bag, every rollout of the sim empties the
    bag within a ply or two. By default the sim then searches that endgame
    (both racks are known once the bag is empty) instead of playing it out
    with static moves, which get the winner wrong in about one close endgame
    in twelve. The search is a quick 2-ply result-only search, capped at
    100 ms, of endgames within 60 points; each endgame is searched once per
    sim and its result reused, since the rollouts keep reaching the same few.
    An endgame whose search runs out of time is played out statically, as
    are all of them with this option set to false. It makes a sim with two
    tiles in the bag about three times slower, and one with one tile in the
    bag about one and a half times slower.

    With three or more tiles in the bag the sim plays its rollouts out
    statically as before: searching there is too slow, and in our tests it
    did not make the bot stronger.

    With one or two tiles in the bag and time to spare, prefer `peg`, which
    solves the pre-endgame exhaustively instead of sampling it.

    -collect-heatmap true

    You can collect a heatmap of a sim if you set this option to true. If you
    do this, make sure to *not* use the sim log command prior. Otherwise,
    the heatmap will not be collected.

    Note that collecting a heatmap has a small performance impact on the sim speed.

    After your sim stops, you can visualize the heatmap and stats of the
    different plays you simmed. Use `sim heatmap` for this. Refer to the beginning
    of this document for more information on the parameters for `sim heatmap`.

    -avoid-prune "F5 FOO"

    Any plays you specify with potentially repeated `-avoid-prune` options
    are added to the sim if they are not in it, and are never cut off. This
    is good for comparing plays you made to the top plays. Under the classic
    and paired rules such a play is rolled out on every iteration. Under bai
    it is rolled out, once the sim has stopped, until it has as many
    rollouts as the most rolled out play, so the sim takes that much longer
    (a sim that hit -maxtime, or that you stopped, skips this).
